### Hindawi CC BY 4.0 does NOT grant audio rights

Hindawi obtained *electronic reading distribution rights* from the Mahfouz and al-Hakim estates. The CC BY 4.0 applies to Hindawi's digital edition (typesetting, formatting), not the underlying literary work. Audio rights and derivative work rights were not part of the deal. For al-Hakim, audio rights are explicitly retained by Dar al-Shorouk.

**Using Hindawi texts to produce commercial audiobooks of copyrighted works is not legally defensible.**

## 2. Distribution Platforms

### Tier 1 — Confirmed AI-friendly, direct upload, no provider restriction

| Platform | AI Policy | Arabic | Revenue | How to publish |
|----------|-----------|--------|---------|---------------|
| **Kobo Writing Life** | Most permissive, no restrictions | Yes | 45% royalty | Upload MP3, label narrator as "Synthesized Voice" |
| **Google Play Books** | Accepts pre-produced AI audio | Yes | 52% revenue share | Upload via Play Books Partner Center |
| **YouTube** | No restrictions on AI narration | Yes | 55% ad revenue (YPP) | Upload as video with audio + cover art |

These three accept audio from any TTS provider (Azure, Google, ElevenLabs). No middleman needed.

### Tier 2 — AI-friendly with constraints

| Platform | AI Policy | Constraint | Revenue |
|----------|-----------|-----------|---------|
| **Spotify / INaudio** | Accepts AI with disclosure | May require ElevenLabs or Google Play production specifically | Aggregator royalty split |
| **PublishDrive** (aggregator) | Partners with ElevenLabs | Can distribute to Apple, Kobo, libraries | Subscription or per-title fee |
| **Author's Republic** (aggregator) | Testing AI acceptance | 50+ platforms | Aggregator split |

INaudio (formerly Findaway Voices) is Spotify's wide-distribution arm. Their AI policy specifically names ElevenLabs and Google Play as accepted production sources.

## 3. Business Paths

### Path A: Public domain demo reel (start here)

**What:** Produce 5-10 public domain Arabic audiobooks (al-Manfaluti, Zaydan, Taha Hussein). Publish on YouTube + Kobo + Google Play.

**Why:** Costs $20-50 total in TTS. Proves the pipeline works end-to-end. Creates a portfolio to show publishers and partners. Tests distribution mechanics.

**Revenue:** Minimal — PD books are free everywhere. Value is the demo, not the sales.

**Timeline:** 1-2 weeks with current pipeline.

### Path B: Service to publishers

**What:** Offer audiobook production as a service to Arabic publishers who have content rights but no audio versions.

**Why:** Publishers have hundreds of books with rights cleared. They can't afford $1,000+/book for human narration. You offer $20-50/book production cost, charge $100-200/book or revenue split.

**Who to target:**
- **Hindawi Foundation** — 3,271 books with electronic rights. Negotiate audio rights extension. They already know the content is clean digital text.
- **Dar al-Shorouk** — Major Egyptian publisher, holds rights to Mahfouz, al-Hakim, and many others.
- **Dar al-Adab** (Beirut) — Literary fiction publisher.
- **Small/indie Arab publishers** — Can't afford human narration, most receptive to AI.

**Revenue model:** Production fee ($100-200/book) or revenue share (50/50 on royalties).

### Path C: Storytel / Arabookverse production partner

**What:** Approach Storytel MENA or Arabookverse as a production partner, not a publisher.

**Pitch:** "You have 5,000+ Arabic titles without audio. I have a two-voice Arabic TTS pipeline that produces quality output at $4-12/book. I can produce your catalog at scale."

**Why this could work:**
- Storytel is actively expanding AI narration but hasn't done Arabic yet.
- Arabookverse just launched Amazon Digital Arabic Library (Jan 2026) and needs content.
- Neither has in-house Arabic AI narration capability.
- Timing is right — both are in expansion mode.

**Revenue model:** Per-book production fee or ongoing production contract.

### Path D: Direct to authors

**What:** Offer affordable audiobook conversion to young Arab authors self-publishing on Amazon KDP.

**Who:** Authors writing in DOCX, publishing ebooks, can't afford human narration. They own their rights.

**Price:** $20-40/book to the author. Your production cost is $4-12.

**Distribution:** Author publishes on Kobo + Google Play + YouTube (you help or they self-serve).

**Revenue:** Production fee margin. Slow but steady.

## 4. Recommended Sequence

```
Phase 0 (Now):      Build PD demo reel — 5-10 books, publish on YouTube/Kobo/Google Play
                     Cost: $20-50 total. Time: 1-2 weeks.

Phase 1 (Month 1):  Pitch Storytel MENA and Arabookverse with demo reel
                     Pitch Hindawi for audio rights extension
                     Goal: 1 production partnership or licensing deal

Phase 2 (Month 2+): Start serving publishers or partner catalog
                     Offer author conversion service ($20-40/book)
                     Build pipeline automation for batch production

Phase 3 (Month 4+): Scale based on which path gets traction
                     Upgrade ElevenLabs plan if quality justifies 3x cost
                     Consider self-serve web tool if author demand validates
```

## 5. Provider Choice Implications for Distribution

| If you produce with... | Direct upload (Kobo, Google Play, YouTube) | INaudio/Spotify | Storytel/Arabookverse |
|------------------------|------------------------------------------|-----------------|----------------------|
| **Azure** | Yes | No (not accepted) | Depends on partner deal |
| **Google Chirp3-HD** | Yes | Unclear | Depends on partner deal |
| **ElevenLabs** | Yes | Yes (explicitly accepted) | Depends on partner deal |

For widest self-distribution: **ElevenLabs** covers all platforms. But the gap only matters for INaudio/Spotify — the three direct-upload platforms accept anything.

For production partnerships (Storytel, Arabookverse, publishers): **provider doesn't matter** — they care about output quality, not which API produced it.
